\section{\pizero{}}
\label{sec:model}
%=============================================================================
\pizero{} is a vision--language--action policy: camera views and an instruction
in, a chunk of future control steps out. It runs as three sequential stages.

The work is spread unevenly (Table~\ref{tab:budget}). The prefix carries three
quarters of the arithmetic and sets the compute bound; the expert is trivial
beside it but is the only sequential stage, so it sets the latency floor.

That expert integrates a learned velocity field over ten Euler steps,
$x_{t+\Delta t}=x_t+\Delta t\,v_\theta(x_t,t,\mathrm{KV}_{\text{prefix}})$, each
attending the cached prefix K/V --- so its error compounds from step to step.

\section{Porting}
\label{sec:port}
%=============================================================================
The \ue{}~v1 is a soft accelerator IP block, here instanced eight times on one
AMD Alveo card with $4$\,GB of attached DRAM. It works on $64$-element alignment,
and those two constraints drive everything below.

\textbf{Weights.} The \bfteen{} checkpoint is several times over the DRAM budget,
so all matmul weights are 4-bit block-quantized --- an $\sim$8$\times$ shrink ---
and dequantized to \bfteen{} just before each MAC. \iffour{} is a footprint
enabler, not a speedup.

\textbf{Vision head-dim pad.} Head-dim $72$ is not $64$-aligned, so the attention
matmuls are padded to $128$ and contracted back through a precomputed selection
matrix; the $Q/K/V/O$ projections stay narrow.

\textbf{Prefix masking.} Dropping the masked image slot compresses prefill by a
quarter, bit-identically. Rotary positions come from cumulative-sum-of-mask
indices rather than \texttt{arange}, so removing a slot does not shift phase.

\textbf{Denoise loop.} All steps unroll into a single program and one execute
call. Every strided SRAM$\to$DRAM copy needs an explicit destination offset; a
missing one scrambles the output without ever producing a NaN, and fixing these
recovered the expert entirely.

\section{Quantization}
\label{sec:quant}
%=============================================================================
\subsection{\iffour{}}
\iffour{} is the accelerator's 4-bit weight format. Every $64$-element block is
quantized both as signed INT4 and as FP4 E2M1, and whichever reconstructs with
lower error is the one kept.

The choice rides in the sign of the block's \bfteen{} scale. That scale is an
absmax quotient and so never negative, which leaves the bit structurally spare:
\iffour{} carries no side table and costs no more than either format alone.

Decode is a $2$:$1$ mux one cycle before the MAC, over paths that already exist
because INT4 and FP4 both ship standalone. The search itself is one-time and
offline.

\textbf{Why the hybrid.} The two grids spend the same number of levels in
opposite ways. INT4 is uniform; FP4 is finer near zero and much coarser out at
the tails, where it leaves a wide gap below its largest code.

So the winner is set by the block's crest factor --- how far its peak sits above
its bulk --- and the crossover is sharp. Dense blocks populate every uniform
level; one outlier stretches the shared scale and collapses the rest.

Table~\ref{tab:if4} makes the case. The extremes go as expected, but the row that
matters is the Gaussian: the two formats tie, and the hybrid still wins, because
crest factor varies block to block inside a single tensor.

That variation is structured. The INT4 share climbs with depth through the vision
tower and bottoms out in the token embedding --- the most heavy-tailed weight in
the model, and the one pure INT4 handles worst.

\subsection{LIBERO}
\label{sec:libero}
LIBERO is a tabletop manipulation benchmark for language-conditioned policies,
scored by closed-loop task success. It supplies two camera views; \pizero{} takes
three, so the spare slot is fed zeros and masked out.

Encoding a slot that contributes nothing costs no correctness and keeps the full
three-slot path exercised, which leaves the port drop-in for embodiments that do
drive a third camera.

An episode is many inferences, not one. The simulator consumes the first ten
steps of each chunk and then asks for another, so every episode is a dozen or
more full vision\,+\,prefix\,+\,denoise passes on hardware.

Table~\ref{tab:libero} runs the FPGA policy against the CUDA reference over ten
prompts, identical seed and fixed denoise noise, so both platforms see
byte-identical observations.

and arithmetic throughput does not help.
